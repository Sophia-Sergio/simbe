## Overrides
1. `UploadModal` follows the **asynchronous** flow of `useSnapshotUpload` (real exports reach 69 MB / ~625k rows): `idle` (dropzone + `FileChecklist`) → `uploading` → `validating` (`UploadProgress`: "Reading your files" → "Checking your data") → `review` ("Review changes": `ReviewReport`; **Replace snapshot** disabled when `report.ok === false`) → `committing` (`UploadProgress`: "Importing rows (24,000 of 52,000)" → "Building your insights"; Cancel is **not** offered once committing) → `done` (toast "Snapshot replaced · no duplicates counted"). Cancel during `validating`/`review` calls `cancel()` (DELETE). **The modal can be closed while a load runs**: the load continues server-side, the page shows a "Loading your data… 46%" card (from `getCurrentSnapshot().pending`), and reopening/reloading resumes the same upload. Replace is single-flight; `409 already_in_progress` → "A load is already running — it will finish on its own."; `failed` → the API's readable `error.message` with a Try again button (previous data stays).
2. While `pending` is non-null, the "CRM exports" card shows the current snapshot (if any) dimmed with a `Loading your data…` strip; otherwise "CRM exports" card: `Loaded <date, time in agency tz>` and the seven rows (green dot, file name, row count) from `getCurrentSnapshot().files`; empty state when `snapshot: null` ("No data loaded yet") with the primary **Upload new snapshot** button.
3. Mocks: `?mock=empty` for the empty state; the default mock walks `upload-ok.json` → `commit.json`; dropping only six files yields `upload-errors.json` (red rows, Replace disabled).
4. After a successful commit the lane relies on the hook's invalidations (no manual refetch).

## Responsive acceptance
- **Desktop:** header with primary button, connector cards side by side, CRM exports card; upload is a centered modal (max 480px).
- **Mobile:** connectors stack; the upload UI is a **bottom sheet** (kit `Modal`), review rows wrap, Replace/Cancel buttons full width in a sticky footer; no horizontal scroll.
